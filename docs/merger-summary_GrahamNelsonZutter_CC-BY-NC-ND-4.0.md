Successfully merged three versions of the Existential Analysis & Logotherapy Four Fundamental Motivations Codebook into a unified **Version 4.0-MERGED**. This comprehensive edition combines:

Three-step process:
1. Recognizing (worth and value)
2. Harmonizing (with inner reality)
3. Giving inner consent (active involvement)

Plus note on omitting the person (leads to reflex, not action)

### 8. **Structure of Will** (v3.3)
Four elements of free and realistic will:
1. Ability and capacity
2. Emotional perception of value
3. Inner permission (life concepts and morality)
4. Meaning (Frankl's contribution)

### 9. **Interpersonal Paradigm** (v1.3)
- Shift from individualistic to interpersonal understanding
- Dialogue as fundamental structure
- Being-with-others vs. individual will

### 10. **Fundamental Motivations** (Comprehensive Merger)
**Structure**: Uses v2.0's systematic organization as base
**Content**: Enhanced with v1.3's assessment questions and v3.3's additional quotes

Each FM includes:
- FM number and name
- Central existential question
- Domain of concern
- Description with supporting quotes
- Psychological question
- Existential needs (3 per FM)
- Motivational force
- Epistemological chain
- Psychological chain
- Mode of experience
- Personal activities (2 per FM)
- Positive outcomes (3+ per FM)
- Negative outcomes (multiple per FM)
- Coping reactions (4 types: basic, activism, aggression, freezing)
- Pathology
- Assessment questions (from v1.3)
- Additional source quotes (from v3.3)

**The Four FMs**:
1. **Being in the World** (Can I be?)
2. **Feeling Life** (Do I like being?)
3. **Being Oneself** (May I be myself?)
4. **Meaning and Future** (What am I here for?)

### 11. **Four Fundamental Realities** (v1.3)
Explicit section connecting the four realities to FMs:
- World in factuality and potentiality → FM1
- Life with relationships and feelings → FM2
- Being oneself as unique, autonomous person → FM3
- Wider context and future development → FM4

### 12. **Cross-Cutting Themes** (v2.0)
Themes that span multiple FMs:
- Personal existential analysis process
- Structure of will  
- Loss of meaning categories
- Meaning definitions (existential, ontological, axiological)

### 13. **Loss of Meaning Categories** (v1.3)
Three categories where meaning is explicitly lost:
- Loss of value (illness, suffering, death, sorrow) → Related to FM2
- Loss of orientation (work losing necessity) → Related to FM4
- Loss of relations (living without relating) → Related to FM3

### 14. **Meaning Characteristics** (v1.3)
Four characteristics of psychological meaning:
1. Objective (situational reference)
2. Relative (to person and linkages)
3. Subjective (recognized by person involved)
4. Appellative (challenging character)
